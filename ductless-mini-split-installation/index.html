Think about the spaces your central system has never handled well: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs too warm or too cold, or an older Wilson home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the spaces a ductless mini split was made for. Nothing travels through leaky ductwork, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a small hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor varies its speed to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it runs as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Design is where mini split projects are won or lost, and Wilson HVAC does it deliberately. Each zone gets sized to its actual load, because an oversized head short-cycles, dehumidifies poorly, and hunts. Multi-zone systems bring their own engineering: the outdoor unit's capacity has to be matched carefully across the connected heads, line set lengths and elevation changes must stay inside the manufacturer's tables, and the choice between wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor units affects both comfort and how the room looks. Our experienced technicians walk the space with you, place heads where the physics and the sightlines agree, and route line sets cleanly, in finished line-hide outdoors.

Installation gets the same rigor as our largest systems. The line set is pressure-tested with nitrogen and evacuated to a micron-gauge-verified vacuum, and flare connections are torqued and leak-checked, since flare joints are where careless installs leak. Condensate is drained or pumped reliably, and the charge is adjusted for line set length per the manufacturer's tables. We commission every zone in both heating and cooling, then hand you the remotes with the settings explained in plain terms. Why Hire a Professional for Ductless Mini Split Installation?

Mini splits look simple hanging on the wall, and that illusion creates a steady stream of failed installs. The flare connections that join the line set are the system's weak point: under-torqued or badly formed flares weep refrigerant for months, and the first symptom is a head blowing lukewarm air a season later. Skipping the nitrogen pressure test and micron-verified vacuum leaves moisture in a circuit that inverter compressors tolerate poorly. Line sets run past the manufacturer's length and lift tables quietly cost capacity, and a condensate drain without proper fall leaves a water stain the first humid week.

Here is the part most installers underestimate: sizing. Even the smallest indoor heads are oversized for plenty of bedrooms, and an oversized head cycles constantly, leaves moisture in the air, and never finds a steady state. Wilson HVAC sizes zones honestly and installs them with the same care, torquing and testing every flare, proving the line set with nitrogen, pulling a measured vacuum, adjusting charge for the run, and testing each head in both modes. The refrigerant side is federally regulated and the electrical side calls for a properly sized circuit and disconnect, so neither is a place to improvise. What you get is a system that stays silent and efficient for fifteen years instead of one that mysteriously underperforms.

Ductless Mini Split Installation Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Clean the washable filter screens in each head about once a month, since they clog faster than furnace filters
  • Give the outdoor unit open space in every season, free of drifts, debris, and mowing clippings
  • Have the indoor blower wheel and coil professionally cleaned before they grow anything
  • Let the inverter do its job: pick a temperature and leave it rather than toggling on and off
  • Brief defrost cycles and a little steam in winter are normal, but a unit encased in ice needs service
  • Check the condensate outlet during cooling season to make sure water is actually coming out
  • Get a yearly professional checkup of charge, flare joints, and drainage while any fix is still small
