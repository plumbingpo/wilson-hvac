Think about the spaces your central system has never handled well: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs hot all summer, or an older Wilson home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the spaces a ductless mini split was made for. Nothing travels through leaky ductwork, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a small hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor modulates to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it runs as a true cold-climate heat pump.

How We Handle Ductless Mini Split Installation

Design is where mini split projects are won or lost, and Wilson HVAC does it deliberately. Each zone gets sized to its actual load, because a head too big for the room cools fast but leaves the air clammy. Multi-zone systems bring their own engineering: the outdoor unit's capacity has to be matched carefully across the connected heads, line set lengths and elevation changes must stay inside the manufacturer's tables, and the choice between wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor units affects both comfort and how the room looks. Our experienced technicians walk every room with you, place heads where the physics and the sightlines agree, and route line sets cleanly, in finished line-hide outdoors.

On installation day, the details that customers never see are the ones we refuse to skip. Every flare is formed carefully, torqued to spec, and leak-checked, because a slowly weeping flare is the number one way mini splits fail. The line set gets a nitrogen pressure test, then a deep vacuum confirmed on a micron gauge, before refrigerant ever moves. We make sure condensate drains with proper fall or a dependable pump, trim the charge for the actual run length, and test each zone in heating and cooling. Why It Matters

The Benefits of Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation

Most failed mini splits we see in Wilson come from installs that treated the job as simple. Refrigerant leaks at the flares are the headline problem: a joint that was poorly formed or never torqued to spec leaks slowly, and the homeowner finds out a season later when the head stops cooling. Moisture is the next issue, because an install that skips the nitrogen pressure test and a micron-verified vacuum leaves water vapor inside a system whose inverter compressor is sensitive to it. Overlong or overly tall line set runs sap capacity quietly, and a drain line without enough fall will stain a wall as soon as humidity climbs.

Here is the part most installers underestimate: sizing. Even the smallest indoor heads are oversized for plenty of bedrooms, and an oversized head cycles constantly, leaves moisture in the air, and never finds a steady state. Wilson HVAC sizes zones honestly and installs them with the same care, torquing and testing every flare, proving the line set with nitrogen, pulling a measured vacuum, adjusting charge for the run, and testing each head in both modes. The refrigerant side is federally regulated and the electrical side calls for a properly sized circuit and disconnect, so neither is a place to improvise. What you get is a system that stays silent and efficient for fifteen years instead of a rushed install that never quite works.

Homeowner Tips

Ductless Mini Split Installation Prevention Tips

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Pull and rinse the indoor filter screens monthly, because they fill up much faster than you would expect
  • Give the outdoor unit open space in every season, free of drifts, debris, and mowing clippings
  • Do not wait for a musty smell; get the blower wheel and coil cleaned by a pro on a regular basis
  • Resist turning the system off and on; an inverter is most efficient holding one steady temperature
  • Brief defrost cycles and a little steam in winter are normal, but a unit encased in ice needs service
  • Check the condensate outlet during cooling season to make sure water is actually coming out
  • Book annual professional service to verify charge, flares, and drainage while fixes are cheap
