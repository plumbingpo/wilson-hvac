Heating and cooling systems rarely fail politely, and in Wilson, NC, a breakdown usually arrives on the hottest afternoon or the coldest night of the year. The tricky part is how many different failures produce the same symptom. A system that runs but never reaches temperature might be fighting a weak run capacitor, a pitted contactor, a low refrigerant charge, a dirty flame sensor, or simply a filter so clogged the blower is starving for air. A system that will not start at all could be a tripped float switch on a backed-up condensate drain, a failed transformer, a bad thermostat, or a control board that has stopped sequencing the equipment.

Every service call from Wilson HVAC starts with testing, not parts. We verify capacitors with a meter, confirm refrigerant charge through superheat and subcooling, measure the microamp signal on flame sensors, and take static pressure readings whenever airflow could be the culprit. Why the rigor? Because a failing capacitor, a hard-starting compressor, and a low-voltage short can look exactly alike, and paying for all three when only one is bad is money wasted. Once the real fault is confirmed, we walk you through what broke, why it broke, and what the repair will take, all before any work begins.

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • The outdoor unit humming but refusing to actually start

Maintenance Tips for HVAC Repair

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Leave every supply and return vent open and free of furniture or rugs
  • Call about unusual noises early, since a squealing bearing costs far less to fix before the motor seizes
  • Give the condenser room to breathe by keeping plants, leaves, and clippings away from it
  • Swap in a fresh filter every one to three months; a clogged one starves the whole system of air
  • Flush the condensate drain line each spring before algae can clog it and trip the float switch
  • During heat waves and cold snaps, set the thermostat a few degrees closer to the outdoor temperature to ease the load
  • Schedule professional maintenance twice a year, before cooling season and before heating season
