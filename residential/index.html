No Surprises

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Before we start any repair, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check the age and tonnage of your system, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. After that you get an honest recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a weak capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

Most HVAC failures give quiet warnings long before the system quits. Keep an eye out for energy bills that creep up, airflow that feels weaker than it used to, frost on the copper lines, or rooms that never catch up to the thermostat. Behind those symptoms are usually pinhole refrigerant leaks, worn blower bearings, or a capacitor on its way out. A quick inspection at the first sign often costs far less than an emergency replacement.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most air conditioners and furnaces last 12 to 18 years, and a planned replacement is always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap during a heat wave or cold snap. Our technicians know the climate demands common to Wilson and how they affect equipment life, so we can tell you honestly whether your system has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Seasonal Tune-Ups

Maintenance Plans for Wilson Homes

Preventing a breakdown beats paying for one. That's the idea behind our maintenance plans. On each scheduled visit, we clean coils and burners, confirm the refrigerant charge, test the electrical components, inspect the heat exchanger, flush the condensate drain, and swap in a new filter, all tailored to the equipment in your home.

Many homes do best with two visits a year: a springtime tune-up for cooling and a fall tune-up for heating. The schedule flexes with the age of your equipment, how hard your household runs it, and local conditions like dust and pollen, which clog condenser coils and raise energy use fast.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a consistent maintenance schedule reduces operating costs. A clean coil transfers heat far more efficiently, a properly charged system stops overworking the compressor, and a fresh filter protects the blower motor. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing an HVAC system even one year early.

Sized for Your Home

Comfort Sized to Your Home's Demand

When it comes to HVAC, bigger is not better. An oversized unit turns on and off too often, wastes energy, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out of the air, while an undersized one runs nonstop and still falls behind on the hottest afternoons. We don't simply copy the tonnage of the old unit. We run a proper load calculation that factors in your square footage, insulation, windows, and ductwork, and for mini-splits we size each head to the room it serves.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising comfort demands. A single-stage system that served a small household rarely satisfies a finished basement and two added bedrooms, and a modern two-stage or variable-speed heat pump can hold tighter temperatures while cutting energy use significantly. If you're weighing furnace-and-AC against a heat pump, or ducted against ductless, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: SEER2 efficiency ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting requirements, and upfront installation work, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

Heating and Cooling Problems We Solve in Wilson

Warm air from the vents, a furnace that won't ignite, a frozen evaporator coil, a slow refrigerant leak: each of these problems has a different cause and needs a different repair. Wilson HVAC tracks down the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's a condenser that hums but won't start, a short-cycling furnace, a thermostat that keeps losing its program, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split throwing error codes.

Not every HVAC problem is equally urgent. A weak capacitor only keeps the compressor from starting. A cracked heat exchanger, on the other hand, can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a backed-up condensate drain can soak through a ceiling before anyone notices. Our technicians carry the parts to swap capacitors, contactors, fan motors, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards, repair leaky ducts that dump conditioned air into the attic, and recharge refrigerant once a leak has been repaired.
