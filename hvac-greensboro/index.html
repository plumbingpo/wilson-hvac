Full-Service HVAC Care for Greensboro Homes and Businesses

We service every type of heating and cooling equipment you'll find in local homes and businesses, from central air conditioners and gas furnaces to heat pumps and ductless mini-splits. It makes no difference whether the building is a single-family house, a condo, or a storefront. Our techs understand the local weather, the age and construction of local homes, and the code requirements that apply to new installations, and that knowledge shows up in every diagnosis.

Most homeowners we meet need one of three things: a repair, a replacement, or a seasonal tune-up. Repairs tend to be straightforward: a burned contactor, a corroded flame sensor, a refrigerant leak, or a seized blower motor. Replacements get a proper load calculation, not a guess based on the old unit's tonnage, which keeps temperatures even and stops the new equipment from short-cycling itself to an early death. Maintenance visits include coil cleaning, a refrigerant charge check, safety control testing, and a static pressure reading, and nothing does more to extend the life of your system.

For commercial property owners and managers nearby, we service the rooftop units, split systems, and makeup air and ventilation equipment that restaurants, retail spaces, offices, and multi-unit buildings depend on. Our commercial team understands that a hot dining room or a freezing office means lost business, so we prioritize fast diagnosis, carry common parts on the truck, and schedule work around your operating hours whenever we can.

Keeping Your Greensboro HVAC System Running Longer

Most mid-season breakdowns trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Swap your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

How old is your system? That number says a lot about what comes next. Most well-maintained air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that point and also shows higher bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or frequent repairs, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement when the first heat wave hits.

Small problems don't stay minor for long. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a few miserable nights.
