From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, everything happens in a strict order: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower sending heat through the house. Diagnosis is mostly a matter of finding where that chain breaks. In Wilson homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Our experienced technicians diagnose a furnace the way it operates, in order, with a meter at every step. That means igniter resistance, microamp flame signal, pressure switch operation under actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor condition. The safety checks come standard on a gas appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak testing on the gas connections, and flue draft verification, since a hazard is not visible at the thermostat. We finish by checking temperature rise against the data plate, making sure the burner and blower are matched the way the manufacturer designed them to.

Wilson HVAC services natural gas, propane, and electric furnaces of every major brand, from standard 80 percent units to condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drain lines, and dual pressure switches. Our trucks carry the parts that actually fail, such as igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so a typical no-heat call ends with the house warming up. Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • The furnace clicks and glows but the burners never light
  • Repeated shutdowns just seconds after the burners light
  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower
  • The blower runs constantly but the air never warms
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint
  • Booming or banging at light-off from delayed ignition
  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue

Why Professional Furnace Repair Matters

Gas, open flame, high voltage, and a flue all share one cabinet in a furnace, and the most dangerous failure is the one you cannot see: a damaged heat exchanger that lets combustion gases into the air the blower circulates. Every morning that delayed ignition keeps booming at startup, that exchanger takes more stress. The diagnostic side has its own pitfalls. When a furnace trips its high limit, the switch itself may be bad, or the furnace may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a failing blower. Swapping the switch in that second case removes a safety device and leaves the actual problem in place.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

How to Prevent Furnace Repair Problems

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
  • Book a fall tune-up so weak igniters and fouled sensors are caught before the first freeze
  • If you have a high-efficiency furnace, check that the condensate drain is flowing freely
  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
