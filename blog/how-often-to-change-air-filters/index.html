How Often Should You Change Your Air Filters in Wilson, NC

Home / Blog / How Often Should You Change Your Air Filters in Wilson, NC

Maintaining a comfortable and healthy home in Wilson, NC often comes down to the little things that make a big difference. One of the most important of these is regularly changing your HVAC system's air filter. It might seem like a minor chore, but a clean air filter is absolutely vital for both your indoor air quality and the efficiency and longevity of your heating and air conditioning system. Let's delve into why this simple task is so important and how often you should be doing it.

Understanding the Role of Your Air Filter

Think of your HVAC air filter as the gatekeeper for your heating and cooling equipment. Its primary job is to snag and retain airborne particles before they can circulate throughout your home or, even worse, accumulate within your HVAC unit itself. These particles can range from common household dust, pet dander, and pollen to mold spores, bacteria, and even some viruses. Without a functioning filter, all of these contaminants would be freely circulating in the air you breathe and settling on surfaces throughout your Wilson home.

Beyond air quality, the filter also protects the delicate internal components of your furnace and air conditioner. A clogged filter restricts airflow. This forced air then has to bypass the filter, carrying all those unwanted particles directly into your system. Over time, this can lead to dirt and debris accumulating on the evaporator coil (in your AC) and the heat exchanger (in your furnace). This buildup not only hinders optimal performance but can also cause serious damage, leading to expensive breakdowns.

Factors Influencing Filter Change Frequency

There's no universal rule to how often you should change your air filter. Several factors play a significant role in determining the ideal frequency for your Wilson home:

  • Occupancy and Activity: A larger household generally means more dust, skin cells, and other particles. If you have a busy household with lots of activity, you'll likely need to change your filter more often.
  • Pets: Furry friends are wonderful companions, but they also contribute significantly to indoor air pollutants through shedding dander and hair. Homes with pets, especially multiple pets, will require more frequent filter changes.
  • Allergies and Respiratory Issues: If anyone in your home suffers from allergies, asthma, or other respiratory conditions, you'll want to prioritize clean air. Changing filters more frequently can help reduce their symptoms and create a healthier environment.
  • Smoking: Cigarette smoke produces microscopic particles that quickly clog filters. If there's smoking inside your home, filters will need to be replaced much more frequently.
  • Construction or Renovations: Renovations, whether inside your home or a neighbor's, kicks up a tremendous amount of dust, drywall particles, and other debris. During and after such projects, you'll want to change your filter more often.
  • Air Pollution Levels: While we aim for clean air in Wilson, external factors like wildfire smoke or local industrial activity can impact the air entering your home.
  • Filter Type: The type of filter you use also matters. Filters with a higher MERV rating capture more particles but can also become clogged faster. We'll discuss MERV ratings in more detail shortly.
  • Seasonality: During peak heating and cooling seasons, your HVAC system runs more frequently, meaning it's moving more air and therefore collecting more particles. In NC, this often means adjusting your schedule for the hottest and coldest months.

General Guidelines for Filter Replacement

While the factors above will fine-tune your schedule, here are some general recommendations:

  • Standard Homes (No Pets, No Allergies): Quarterly is a good baseline.
  • Homes with One Pet or Mild Allergies: Bi-monthly is often recommended.
  • Homes with Multiple Pets or Severe Allergies/Asthma: Consider changing your filter monthly or every six weeks.
  • During Peak Seasons (Heavy Usage): If your system is running almost constantly in the heat of summer or cold of winter, a monthly check-up is a good idea.
  • Vacant Homes/Seldom Used HVAC: Even in homes that aren't occupied regularly, it's still a good idea to change the filter at least twice a year to prevent dust buildup.

The best advice? Make it a habit to inspect your filter monthly. If it looks dirty, change it. It's a quick, easy task that can save you a lot of hassle and money down the road.

Understanding MERV Ratings

When you go to buy a new air filter, you'll notice a MERV rating on the packaging. MERV stands for Minimum Efficiency Reporting Standard, and it's a system developed by the American Society of Heating, Refrigerating and Air-Conditioning Engineers (ASHRAE) to rate the effectiveness of air filters. The higher the MERV rating, the more microscopic particles the filter can capture.

  • MERV 1-4 (Disposable Fiberglass Filters): These are the most basic filters. They primarily protect your HVAC equipment from large dust particles but offer very little in terms of improving indoor air quality.
  • MERV 5-8 (Pleated Filters): A good balance of filtration and airflow. These filters effectively capture pollen, dust mites, mold spores, and pet dander. Most homeowners find these to be a good compromise.
  • MERV 9-12 (High-Efficiency Pleated Filters): These filters offer enhanced filtration, capturing smaller particles like auto emissions, lead dust, and some bacteria. If you have allergies, these are a solid choice.
  • MERV 13-16 (Superior Filtration Filters): These filters are very effective at removing airborne viruses, bacteria, and smoke particles. However, they can restrict airflow more, so it's crucial to consult with an HVAC professional like Wilson HVAC before using them, especially in older systems, to ensure your system can handle the increased resistance.
  • MERV 17-20 (HEPA Filters): These are hospital-grade filters, capable of trapping nearly all airborne particles. They typically require specialized HVAC systems due to their significant airflow restriction.

For most residential homes in Wilson, a MERV rating between 5 and 12 is appropriate. Going too high without professional advice can actually harm your system by restricting airflow too much, leading to reduced efficiency and potential equipment damage. If you're unsure which MERV rating is right for your system, Wilson HVAC can help.

The Impact of Dirty Filters on Your HVAC System and Wallet

A dirty air filter isn't just an inconvenience; it can have significant negative consequences for your HVAC system and your finances:

Increased Energy Bills: When a filter is clogged, your HVAC system has to work much harder to pull air through it. This increased effort means your furnace or air conditioner consumes more energy, leading to spiking electricity or gas costs. You could be wasting 5-15% of your energy just because of a dirty filter.

Reduced System Efficiency: Beyond just energy consumption, the overall efficiency of your system declines. It won't be able to heat or cool your home as effectively, meaning it will run longer cycles to reach the desired temperature, further exacerbating energy waste.

Decreased Lifespan of Equipment: The constant strain on your HVAC components due to restricted airflow can lead to premature wear and tear. Parts like the blower motor can overheat and fail, leading to costly replacements and a reduced service life for your entire system.

Poor Indoor Air Quality: The most obvious impact is on the air you breathe. A dirty filter can no longer effectively trap pollutants, meaning more dust, allergens, and other irritants are circulating throughout your home. This can worsen existing health conditions for occupants.

System Breakdowns: In severe cases, a very dirty filter can cause your system to completely break down. For example, a clogged filter can cause the evaporator coil in your air conditioner to freeze over, leading to a complete loss of cooling. Similarly, it can cause a furnace to overheat.

How to Change Your Air Filter (A Step-by-Step Guide)

Changing your air filter is one of the easiest yet most effective homeowner tasks you can perform. Here’s how:

  1. Locate Your Filter: Air filters are usually found in one of two places: in the return air duct, often in a wall or ceiling grill, or directly at the air handler or furnace unit itself. In Wilson, it's most common to find them at the furnace.
  2. Turn Off Your System: Safety first! Before doing any work on your HVAC system, turn off the power at the thermostat and, if possible, at the main electrical breaker for the unit.
  3. Note the Size and MERV Rating: On the frame of your old filter, you’ll see numbers indicating its dimensions (e.g. 20x25x1) and its MERV rating. Make sure your new filter matches these specifications. Also, note the arrow indicating airflow direction.
  4. Remove the Old Filter: Depending on its location, you might need to unlatch a grill or slide the filter out of a dedicated slot.
  5. Insert the New Filter: Slide the new filter into place, ensuring the airflow arrow on the filter points in the direction of the airflow towards the furnace/air handler. Getting this right is important.
  6. Secure and Restore Power: Close any latches or access panels, then turn the power back on to your HVAC system at the breaker and thermostat.
  7. Dispose of the Old Filter: Wrap it up and discard it. You've just improved your home's air quality and protected your investment!
